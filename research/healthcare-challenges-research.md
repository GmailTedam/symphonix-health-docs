# Healthcare System Challenges Research: Six-Country Analysis (2024-2026)

Research conducted March 2026. All findings sourced from web searches of current publications.

---

## 1. GHANA

### Health System Structure & Key Reforms
- The National Health Insurance Authority (NHIA) administers the National Health Insurance Scheme (NHIS), which provides coverage through district-wide mutual health insurance schemes
- The Community-based Health Planning and Services (CHPS) system is the lowest level of care delivery, with digital rollout progressing from national level down to community level
- The Ghana Health Service (GHS) operates the District Health Information Management System (DHIMS-2) for public health reporting, while the Lightwave Health Information Management System (LHIMS) handles patient-level records
- The Health Information System Strategic Plan 2022-2025 guides digital transformation efforts

### Challenge 1: Fragmented Digital Systems and Lack of Interoperability
DHIMS functions as a public health-level system while LHIMS operates at the patient level, but there is no interoperability between these two systems, leading to data duplication. Over 60% of health facilities still rely on manual, paper-based workflows for clinical documentation, billing, and procurement. Staff must manually tally data for the national DHIMS-2 platform, leading to errors and duplicated effort.

### Challenge 2: NHIS Digital Claims Failures
NHIA's move toward electronic claims demands structured data formats, but many facilities lack tools that standardize entries or export data into acceptable formats. Failure to align systems with NHIA protocols results in continued claim rejections despite digitization. The disconnect between facility-level systems and NHIA's claims platform creates a bottleneck in reimbursement.

### Challenge 3: Digital Health Infrastructure Crisis
Ghana's digital health system faces a governance crisis. In late 2024, Lightwave's contract with the Ministry of Health expired and was not renewed, putting the national health data system at risk. The Data Protection Commission launched an investigation into Lightwave's handling of national health data. Intermittent internet connectivity, frequent power outages, and limited user training continue to hinder digital adoption.

### Challenge 4: Healthcare Workforce Drain and Capacity Gaps
Africa-wide, there are only 1.55 health workers per 1,000 people versus the WHO-recommended 4.45. Ghana's Ministry of Health hosted a National Policy Dialogue on Health Workforce Transformation in April 2025, launching a Health Labour Market Analysis highlighting gaps in workforce supply, demand, and absorption capacity. Migration of healthcare professionals to developed countries exacerbates shortages.

### Challenge 5: Outdated Data Protection and AI Governance
Ghana's Data Protection Act of 2012 (Act 843) predates AI, digital health, and modern cross-border data flows. The government is developing a new Data Protection Bill to regulate AI systems, automated decision-making, and cross-border data transfers, but this is not yet enacted. There is no health-data-specific regulatory framework.

### Regulatory Framework
- **Data Protection**: Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843); new bill under development (announced March 2026)
- **Health Data**: No dedicated health data law; governed under general DPA
- **AI Governance**: National AI Strategy under consultation; no enacted AI legislation

## 2. UNITED KINGDOM (NHS England)

### Health System Structure & Key Reforms
- The NHS operates through 42 Integrated Care Systems (ICSs), each with an Integrated Care Board (ICB) responsible for strategic commissioning
- The NHS 10-Year Health Plan identifies 5 transformative technologies: data, AI, genomics, wearables, and robotics
- In October 2024, the government announced plans for a single patient record accessible through the NHS App by 2028
- The Data (Use and Access) Act received Royal Assent in June 2025, reforming NHS data standards

### Challenge 1: Fragmented EPR Systems and Interoperability Gaps
The NHS is a complex federation of trusts with legacy IT systems, creating digital silos where a patient's primary care GP records are disconnected from hospital specialist imaging data. The picture of integrated and interoperable EPR varies greatly across the country. AI implementation is hampered because it thrives on large, integrated datasets that the current fragmentation prevents. The single patient record initiative (target: 2028) is an acknowledgment that this problem remains unsolved.

### Challenge 2: GP Access Crisis and Workforce Decline
Between 2012 and 2023, patients finding it easy to get a GP appointment fell from 81% to 50%. FTE GP partner numbers fell from 15,669 in January 2025 to 15,223 in January 2026 -- a loss of 436 FTE GP partners in twelve months. A 2025 BMA survey found 56% of GPs wanted more NHS hours, while 15% could not find suitable GP work at all, indicating a maldistribution problem. The NHS faces a projected 250,000 workforce gap by 2030.

### Challenge 3: Record Waiting Lists and Backlogs
The waiting list stood at 7.25 million cases (approximately 6.13 million individual patients) in January 2026. Median waiting time for treatment was 13.6 weeks, up from a pre-COVID median of 7.8 weeks. A&E queues had swelled to over 100 people on a typical evening by 2024, compared to under 40 in 2009.

### Challenge 4: AI Governance and Implementation at Scale
While AI shows transformational potential (up to 90% faster radiotherapy planning, 73% reduction in patient waiting times, potential 12.5 billion GBP annual savings), challenges include algorithmic bias, data governance, ensuring patient trust, and workforce readiness. The government has signaled it will introduce AI-specific legislation, a shift from its earlier sector-led approach. Scaling AI across 42 different ICS configurations with inconsistent data quality remains a major barrier.

### Challenge 5: ICS Strategic Capacity
ICB leaders need confidence and support from the center to make strategic decisions. Cost reduction requirements compete with the need for effective commissioning. The tension between central NHS directives and local ICS autonomy creates governance complexity for digital transformation and AI adoption.

### Regulatory Framework
- **Data Protection**: UK GDPR + Data Protection Act 2018; Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (phased implementation)
- **Health Data**: NHS-specific data standards reformed under DUA Act 2025; Caldicott principles
- **AI Governance**: No AI-specific legislation yet; AI-specific bill under development; NHS AI Lab operational

## 3. IRELAND

### Health System Structure & Key Reforms
- The Health Service Executive (HSE) is the sole provider/commissioner of public health services
- Slaintecare (launched 2017) is a cross-party 10-year plan for universal healthcare, now refreshed as "Slaintecare 2025+" with 23 individual reform programmes
- Six new HSE Health Regions commenced in March 2024, with Regional Executive Officers accountable for integrated care delivery
- "Digital for Care" framework (2024-2030) published May 2024 as the national digital health strategy

### Challenge 1: Lack of National Shared Care Record and Digital Fragmentation
Ireland currently lacks a unified national shared care record. A health service mobile app, national shared care record, and virtual wards are among priority digital projects for 2025 and beyond. The HSE experienced a devastating ransomware attack in 2021, and rebuilding secure digital infrastructure remains ongoing. Strong governance, interoperability standards, and legislation are identified as key enablers that are not yet fully in place.

### Challenge 2: Persistent Waiting Lists Despite Investment
HSE missed targets on hospital admissions and waiting lists for treatments. Significant funding of 420 million EUR was allocated to the Waiting List Action Plan 2025 (190M EUR for HSE, 230M EUR for NTPF). There has been only a 1% reduction in patients waiting over 12 months year-on-year, and a 10% reduction in those waiting over 18 months. Capacity constraints in public hospitals remain acute.

### Challenge 3: Severe Workforce Shortages and Retention Failure
Up to 120 cancer patients per day are not receiving treatment due to shortage of radiation therapists, with staffing 30% below required levels across five public radiotherapy centers. Over 50% of non-EU recruited healthcare staff leave within 18 months. The system is increasingly reliant on international recruitment, yet attrition undermines these gains.

### Challenge 4: Regional Integration Under New HSE Health Regions
The transition to six Health Regions (March 2024) requires new governance, data sharing, and coordination mechanisms that are still being established. Integrating care across hospital groups, community services, and primary care within each region requires digital infrastructure that does not yet exist. The Slaintecare vision of shifting care from hospitals to communities requires population health data capabilities that are in early stages.

### Challenge 5: EU AI Act Compliance and Health AI Deployment
The EU AI Act (entered into force August 2024) applies directly to Ireland, with high-risk AI system rules (including healthcare applications) commencing August 2026. Ireland's National AI Strategy was refreshed in November 2024 ("AI -- Here for Good"). The Oireachtas Joint Committee on AI published 85 recommendations for Ireland's AI future. Healthcare AI deployment must navigate GDPR, the AI Act, and the not-yet-enacted National Cyber Security Bill.

### Regulatory Framework
- **Data Protection**: EU GDPR (directly applicable); Data Protection Acts 1988-2018; DPC is the regulator
- **Health Data**: No dedicated health data legislation; governed under GDPR with sectoral codes
- **AI Governance**: EU AI Act (phased from 2024-2027); National AI Strategy Refresh 2024; 85 Oireachtas recommendations

## 4. KENYA

### Health System Structure & Key Reforms
- In October 2023, Kenya enacted four landmark health laws: the Social Health Insurance Act, Primary Healthcare Act, Facility Improvement Financing Act, and Digital Health Act
- The Social Health Authority (SHA) replaced the National Health Insurance Fund (NHIF), overseeing three funds: SHIF, Primary Healthcare Fund, and Emergency/Chronic/Critical Illness Fund
- Healthcare is devolved to 47 counties under the 2010 Constitution
- The Digital Health Act designates health data as a strategic national asset

### Challenge 1: SHA/SHIF Transition Payment Crisis
One year after launching SHA in October 2024, the system presents a paradox of millions registered but healthcare on the brink of financial collapse. Only 20% of Primary Healthcare facilities received payments every month during January-March 2025. 45% received no PHC payments in that quarter. 83% of Level 3-5 facilities reported less than half of submitted claims were paid. The overall SHIF claims settlement rate averaged just 34%.

### Challenge 2: Digital Divide and Biometric Verification Failures
The NHIF-to-SHIF transition caused disruptions due to technical failures including biometric verification and claims processing issues. The digital divide is wide, particularly in rural areas where access to electricity, internet, and digital literacy is limited. Gender-based barriers persist among women in rural areas. Elderly and disabled individuals struggle with digital health services due to lack of accessible platforms and assistive technologies.

### Challenge 3: County Devolution Coordination Failures
National-County coordination needs strengthening to align roles, resources, and responsibilities under devolved healthcare. The 47 counties have varying capacity to implement digital health systems and process claims. Inconsistent data systems across counties prevent coherent national health intelligence. The gap between national policy ambition (SHA, Digital Health Act) and county-level implementation capacity is severe.

### Challenge 4: Informal Sector Coverage Gap
Approximately 66% of the working population is in the informal sector, posing a barrier to mandatory health insurance implementation. Low health insurance uptake persists despite the mandate. Digital enrollment mechanisms are inaccessible to many in the informal economy.

### Challenge 5: AI Governance Gap Despite Progressive Digital Health Law
Kenya's Digital Health Act 2023 is among the most comprehensive in Africa, but it does not have AI-specific legislation. There is no regulation of derived datasets or synthetic data generated through AI analysis of protected aggregated health data. The Digital Health (Health Information Management Procedures) Regulations 2025 provide implementation detail but do not address AI governance. Kenya's Data Protection Act (2019) provides general coverage but lacks health-AI specificity.

### Regulatory Framework
- **Data Protection**: Data Protection Act 2019 (Cap. 411C)
- **Health Data**: Digital Health Act 2023; Digital Health Regulations 2025; health data designated as strategic national asset
- **AI Governance**: No AI-specific legislation; gaps in AI-derived data regulation

## 5. NIGERIA

### Health System Structure & Key Reforms
- Three-tier system: federal (tertiary), state (secondary), local government (primary healthcare)
- National Health Insurance Authority (NHIA) Act 2022 replaced the former NHIS, making insurance mandatory
- Basic Health Care Provision Fund (BHCPF) provides financing for primary healthcare
- National Digital Health Initiative (NDHI) endorsed by National Council on Health in November 2024
- Nigeria adopted 32 ISO/TC215 Health Informatics Standards

### Challenge 1: Extreme Fragmentation Across 36 States + FCT
Referral pathways between tiers are inconsistent, and patient records almost never follow a patient across facilities. Each state has its own health systems with varying digital maturity. The NDHI aims to unify platforms (DHIS2, SORMAS, NHIA systems, LMIS, EMRs) into a cohesive national architecture, but implementation is nascent. Fragmentation, limited institutional ownership, and donor-dependency remain systemic.

### Challenge 2: Catastrophic Out-of-Pocket Spending Despite NHIA
Out-of-pocket spending reached 75.1% of total health expenditure -- among the highest globally. NHIA coverage remains below 5% of the total population despite the 2022 mandate. Over 20 million Nigerians are enrolled with an ambitious target of 44 million by 2030 (in a population of 220+ million). Even among enrollees, 70% still finance health needs independently due to poor service delivery, limited coverage scope, and frequent stockouts.

### Challenge 3: Healthcare Brain Drain Crisis
Nearly half of Nigerian medical graduates emigrate within 15 years of qualification. Physician density is only 3.8 per 10,000 people. The National Policy on Health Workforce Migration (August 2024) aims to create retention incentives and attract diaspora returns. Insufficient training in digital health tools, internet connectivity issues, and poor technical support further weaken workforce capacity.

### Challenge 4: Informal Sector Coverage (76.7% of Workforce)
An estimated 76.7% of Nigerians work in the informal sector. The GIFSHIP program under NHIA extends coverage to the informal sector but uptake remains low. Community-Based Health Insurance (CBHI) models exist but face sustainability challenges. Digital enrollment and claims processing is inaccessible to most informal workers.

### Challenge 5: Digital Health Maturity and AI Readiness
A sub-national assessment across 10 states found highly variable digital health maturity. Healthcare professionals face challenges from insufficient digital training, connectivity issues, and poor technical support. Nigeria does not yet have a dedicated AI Act. The National Artificial Intelligence Strategy is expected to be finalized between late 2025 and early 2026. The NDPA 2023 provides general data protection but lacks health-AI-specific provisions.

### Regulatory Framework
- **Data Protection**: Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) 2023; Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC)
- **Health Data**: No dedicated health data law; NHIA Act 2022 governs insurance data; NDHI being established
- **AI Governance**: No AI Act; National AI Strategy expected late 2025/early 2026; aligned with AU Continental AI Strategy 2024

## 6. RWANDA

### Health System Structure & Key Reforms
- Community-Based Health Insurance (CBHI/Mutuelle de Sante) covers 83.5% of the population as of early 2025
- Approximately 10% of the population remains uninsured
- Rwanda Health Sector Strategic Plan V (2024-2029) guides the health sector
- The Rwanda Health Information Exchange (RHIE) implements the "One Patient, One Record" vision
- Transition to capitation for PHC began in November 2025 in the Eastern Province

### Challenge 1: CBHI Financial Sustainability and Premium Barriers
CBHI has faced chronic financial deficit since 2011/2012. Economic constraints prevent individuals from affording premiums, deductibles, and co-payments. The transition to capitation (rolling out from November 2025) is an attempt to improve financial sustainability but introduces new complexities. Coverage scope expansion (kidney transplants, cancer treatment, assistive devices) increases fiscal pressure.

### Challenge 2: Geographic Access and Infrastructure Gaps
Rwanda's hilly terrain and inefficient transport infrastructure make physical access to health facilities challenging. Drug stockouts persist, particularly at lower-level facilities. Rural areas face limited internet access, which hampers digital health system usage. Infrastructure improvements are needed to support the digital health vision beyond urban centers.

### Challenge 3: Digital Literacy and Workforce Capacity
Digital literacy, especially among older healthcare workers, remains a barrier to digital health adoption. Community health workers (3 per village) form the backbone of primary care but need digital tools and training. RHIA is developing a Digital Health Academy offering courses on Health Informatics, Data Privacy, AI for Health, and Interoperability, but this is forthcoming. Ensuring the 45,000+ community health workers can effectively use digital tools is a massive training challenge.

### Challenge 4: Interoperability and System Integration Maturity
The RHIE uses interoperability standards (OpenHIE-aligned) for facility systems to communicate, making Rwanda a regional leader. However, ensuring interoperability across various systems remains complex and resource-intensive. The 2024 Model Law on Health Data Governance Pilot identified gaps in consent, access, and interoperability. Scaling from a functioning HIE to a fully integrated, real-time national health data platform requires sustained investment.

### Challenge 5: AI Governance and Cross-Border Data Sharing
Rwanda has a national AI policy (developed with C4IR Rwanda) and is among the first African nations with such a framework. The Law Relating to the Protection of Personal Data and Privacy (2021) requires breach notification within 48 hours and explicit consent for sensitive data. The National Data Sharing Policy (May 2025) outlines phased implementation from 2025-2029, including data governance unit establishment and standards definition. Cross-border health data sharing with EAC partners lacks harmonized frameworks. Babyl delivers 5,000+ virtual consultations daily using AI triage, but governance of AI clinical decisions at this scale needs stronger oversight.

### Regulatory Framework
- **Data Protection**: Law Relating to the Protection of Personal Data and Privacy (2021); 48-hour breach notification
- **Health Data**: RHIE operational; National Data Sharing Policy (May 2025); health data governance pilot 2024
- **AI Governance**: National AI Policy (with C4IR Rwanda); no dedicated AI Act

## CROSS-CUTTING THEMES RELEVANT TO PLATFORM OPPORTUNITY

### 1. Health Information Exchange (HIE)
- **Ghana**: No functional HIE; DHIMS and LHIMS are siloed
- **UK**: Fragmented EPR across trusts; single patient record targeted for 2028
- **Ireland**: No national shared care record; priority project under Digital for Care 2024-2030
- **Kenya**: Digital Health Act provides legal basis but implementation is nascent
- **Nigeria**: NDHI aims to unify platforms but is at early stage across 36+ states
- **Rwanda**: RHIE operational and most mature of the six countries

### 2. AI Governance Gaps
- No country in this group has enacted comprehensive health-AI-specific legislation
- Rwanda and the UK are furthest along in policy development
- Ghana, Nigeria, and Kenya are developing national AI strategies but have not enacted them
- Ireland must comply with the EU AI Act but implementation guidance for health is pending

### 3. Claims/Insurance Processing Pain Points
- **Ghana**: NHIS claim rejections due to format misalignment
- **Kenya**: 34% SHIF claims settlement rate; biometric verification failures
- **Nigeria**: 70% of insured still pay out of pocket due to poor service delivery
- **Rwanda**: CBHI chronic deficit; transitioning to capitation model
- **UK**: Not claims-based but faces massive procedural backlogs
- **Ireland**: 420M EUR invested in waiting list reduction with marginal improvement

### 4. Workforce Challenges Technology Could Address
- All six countries face workforce shortages (brain drain in Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya; retention in Ireland, UK)
- AI triage and virtual consultations (Rwanda's Babyl model) could extend workforce capacity
- Digital tools for community health workers could improve data collection and care quality
- Remote consultation infrastructure could address geographic access barriers

### 5. Cross-Border/Cross-Regional Coordination
- **West Africa (Ghana/Nigeria)**: No harmonized health data frameworks across ECOWAS
- **East Africa (Kenya/Rwanda)**: EAC lacks health data sharing standards
- **UK/Ireland**: Common Travel Area creates unique cross-border health considerations; both navigating distinct data protection regimes (UK GDPR vs EU GDPR)
- **Pan-African**: AU Continental AI Strategy 2024 provides aspirational alignment but no binding interoperability standards
